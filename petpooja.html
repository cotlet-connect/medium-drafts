How to connect Petpooja to Claude or ChatGPT and stop doing it by hand

Petpooja has no MCP server and publishes no API documentation for restaurants (Checked 31 Oct 2026), so an AI agent cannot plug into it directly. The route is Cotlet.dev, which lets ChatGPT, Claude or any other agent work inside your own Petpooja login. Below are the problem, the four steps, and one job from start to finish.

The problem owners of Petpooja run into

Petpooja is built to run the restaurant: billing, menu, stock, reports and online orders across your outlets. It is not built to be asked questions by an AI agent. When you want sales and tax for every outlet, the answer sits in the back office, one outlet at a time, and you collect it by hand.

Petpooja does have an integrations page, but it says "Become our integration partner" and offers a "Contact us" button. The addresses developer.petpooja.com and api.petpooja.com showed nothing when checked on 31 Oct 2026. That route is meant for partners Petpooja agrees to, and an owner who just wants an agent to read a report is not one of them.

Petpooja's pricing page does not help either. It lists Core, Growth and Scale with feature lists and no price, and every plan has a "Book A Demo" button and no line about an API. If you have wondered why ChatGPT cannot simply be pointed at your restaurant data, this is why.

How to connect Petpooja to an AI agent

Cotlet.dev takes a different path. Your agent works in Petpooja the way you would, using a login you control, and Cotlet.dev provides the tools the agent calls. Four steps get you there.

1. Sign up at Cotlet.dev.

2. Sign in to Petpooja in the Cloud Browser. You type the password yourself, and Cotlet.dev never sees it.

3. Press Enable for agents on Petpooja.

4. Add https://cotlet.dev/mcp/v1 as an MCP server in your agent, whether that is ChatGPT, Claude or any other agent that supports MCP.

Petpooja decides what any login can do, and the tools can only do what that login can do. Use your own login at billing.petpooja.com, either as an owner or as a user whose rights cover reports, menu and inventory. Petpooja sets rights per user, so a sensible habit is to give the agent a login that covers only the outlets and screens it needs.

Cotlet.dev also depends on your plan. Petpooja's pricing page lists the Inventory Module, Menu management and 80+ reports among its plan features, and shows no price. Ask Petpooja which plan includes each module before you expect the agent to reach it.

One job, start to finish

Petpooja pays you through Swiggy and Zomato, and checking that the money matches the orders is a chore most owners do rarely or not at all. With the connection in place, you can type something like "Check my Swiggy and Zomato payouts for the Indiranagar outlet and tell me if anything looks off" into your agent. That wording is only an illustration, and you can ask in your own words.

Your AI agent starts by listing the outlets on your account, so it knows which one you mean and whether online ordering is on. It then pulls the online order reconciliation for that outlet. For each platform that report shows orders, order value, commission, charges, taxes and the expected payout.

Your AI agent reads without pausing, because reading changes nothing in your restaurant. It can put the platforms side by side, point out where commission and charges eat into the payout, and answer follow-up questions in plain language. You can then ask it to review cancelled and discounted bills for the same outlet. Those come with the name of the person who did them, so you see who rather than just how many.

Petpooja's pages give you a quick way to see what is possible beyond that: the integration page at Cotlet.dev lists every Petpooja tool, so you can read the full tool list and the comparison table there. The tools cover reports, menu, stock, purchase orders and the CRM. Kitchen display, captain ordering, the restaurant website and loyalty add-ons have no tool, and Petpooja documents no webhooks for owners.

What waits for your OK

Your AI agent asks before it does anything that reaches other people. Changing the price of a dish waits for your OK. Anything that touches what your customers see online, such as switching an item or a whole outlet on or off, is the kind of change you should expect to confirm first.

Petpooja says menu changes, prices and item switches made in its POS update across the aggregators connected to your outlet, as stated on its online order management page (checked 31 Oct 2026). Cotlet.dev makes the change in Petpooja the way you would, so the same sync applies. Your agent cannot see the Swiggy or Zomato screens, so it works from Petpooja alone.
