How to connect Booksy to Claude or ChatGPT and stop doing it by hand

Booksy has a partner-only Public API and a ChatGPT plugin for clients, but no MCP server and no API key an owner can get (Checked 24 Oct 2026). The route that works for a shop owner is Cotlet.dev, which lets ChatGPT, Claude or any other AI agent work inside your own Booksy Biz account. Below are the four steps to connect it and one request to try.

The problem: Booksy is built for your hands

Booksy Biz is a good app for running a shop by hand. You open the calendar, move a client, block a lunch break, read the new reviews. The trouble starts when you want an AI agent to do part of that with you, because Booksy gives an owner no door to knock on.

Booksy does have a Public API. It covers appointments, customers, services, staff schedules, time off and reviews, plus one appointment webhook. But the docs at docs.booksy.com answer 401, and the keys are RSA pairs that Booksy issues to approved partners. A shop owner cannot ask for one.

Booksy also has a ChatGPT plugin, dated 19 Oct 2026. It lets US clients find a pro and pick a live slot. ChatGPT sees your open slots and service names, nothing else, and you cannot act on your shop through it. That link points toward your customers, and it only reads.

Booksy has no app on the usual automation tools either. Stats & Reports and Message Blasts sit in no official route at all, so revenue per staff member, your blasts and your review replies stay behind your login.

The way in

Cotlet.dev is the way around that wall. It is unofficial and not affiliated with Booksy. You sign in to your own Booksy Biz account, and your agent gets Booksy tools: calendar and appointments, staff and time off, client cards, Message Blasts, reviews and reports from Stats & Reports.

Cotlet.dev tests the Booksy tools every 12 h, and 29/29 checks passed on Nov 6, 2026. When Booksy changes its app, the fix happens on the Cotlet.dev side, so you do not maintain a connection yourself.

Connect in four steps

Cotlet.dev needs four steps, and none of them asks Booksy for a key:

1. Sign up at Cotlet.dev.

2. Sign in to Booksy Biz in the Cloud Browser. You type the password yourself, and Cotlet.dev never sees it.

3. Press Enable for agents on Booksy.

4. Add https://cotlet.dev/mcp/v1 as an MCP server in your agent, whether that is ChatGPT, Claude or any other agent that supports MCP.

Booksy stays signed in inside the Cloud Browser, so the password remains with you and the agent never handles it. Once the address from step 4 is added, the agent lists the Booksy tools and can use them when you ask.

Cotlet.dev shows every Booksy tool, the comparison with the partner API and the plugin, and the latest checks on the Booksy API and MCP server page. A longer written walkthrough of the same connection is in the Cotlet.dev guide to connecting Booksy to ChatGPT and Claude, if you want a second look at a step.

One request, start to finish

Your AI agent works from plain words. On a morning when a barber calls in sick, you type something like "Review tomorrow: one of my barbers is out all day, find new times for his clients and block his calendar."

Your AI agent starts with reads, and reads do not need your approval. It lists tomorrow's appointments with client, service, staff and time. It checks the staff list and working hours to see who offers the same services. Then it looks for free start times, which come from working hours, bookings, blocks and time off.

Your AI agent comes back with a proposal rather than a finished job. You see each affected appointment next to a suggested new time or another staff member, and you can change any line before agreeing.

Your AI agent does the moving only after you say yes. It reschedules each appointment to the time you agreed and adds time off to the barber's calendar so no one can book it. Afterwards you open the calendar, see the blocked day and the moved appointments, and judge for yourself whether the agent understood you.

What waits for your OK

Booksy changes that reach other people wait for your OK by default. Booking, moving and cancelling appointments, Message Blasts and review replies all pause until you confirm. A Message Blast also needs a paid Booksy plan, because Booksy refuses one-time blasts before that, and you confirm each blast before it goes out.

Booksy client cards, private notes and time off change nothing a client sees, so those do not need the same pause. The Booksy tools do not read or answer client chat, confirm or decline booking requests, mark no-shows, take payments or deposits, schedule a blast for later, or turn automated blasts on and off. Those stay in your hands inside Booksy.

Your AI agent reads without pausing and asks before anything reaches your clients. That is the line to hold when you set it up: let it look at everything, and keep the final yes for every appointment it moves, every blast it sends and every reply it posts under your shop's name.
